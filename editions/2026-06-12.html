COP31 Action Agenda lands at Bonn: "35 by 35" electrification target headlines the first tripartite presidency in COP history

On June 9, at the Bonn mid-year climate talks, COP31 President-Designate Murat Kurum and Australia's chief negotiator Chris Bowen unveiled the headline architecture for November's summit in Antalya: a global target to lift electricity's share of final energy consumption from roughly 20% today to 35% by 2035 (built on IRENA analysis), a goal to halve the growth of global waste by 2035, and a 25% reduction in building-sector energy intensity by 2035. The launch operationalizes the unprecedented arrangement struck after Belém — Türkiye hosts and holds the presidency, Australia holds exclusive authority over negotiations, and the Pacific hosts a special pre-COP in Fiji and Tuvalu on October 5–8. Within 48 hours, more than 150 global leaders and experts pressed the presidency in an open letter to make Antalya a "Blue COP" with the ocean at the centre.

Structurally, this matters in two directions. First, the Action Agenda quietly shifts COP31's centre of gravity from negotiated text to sectoral targets — a presidency-led, plurilateral model that rewards coalitions of the willing and sidelines the consensus chokepoints that exhausted Belém. For Global South delegations, that is double-edged: electrification "35 by 35" speaks directly to energy access in Africa and South Asia, but target-setting without finance plumbing repeats a familiar pattern — the Baku-to-Belém Roadmap's $1.3 trillion architecture remains the unfunded counterpart to every new number. Second, the tripartite presidency is a constitutional novelty in the UNFCCC: a fractured hosting contest resolved by distributing sovereignty across two states and a region. It hands the Pacific institutional leverage it has never held — pre-COP hosting, a leaders' moment in Tuvalu — and creates a precedent every future contested presidency will cite. This story connects directly to the Pacific's ocean-agenda push, the post-Belém finance roadmap, Brazil's forest-data diplomacy, and the state-led energy transition debate now running through the Global South.

Why this story won: It was independently covered across presidency channels, wire services, regional African and Turkish outlets, and specialist climate media (criterion 1); it appears as context inside the Pacific, finance, energy-transition and Brazil stories in this scan (criterion 2); the announcements landed June 9–11 and consultations are live now (criterion 3); it changes how COP presidencies are constituted and how agendas are set (criterion 4); and it binds Africa, Asia-Pacific, Latin America and MENA into a single agenda-setting moment (criterion 5).

Deforestation data becomes foreign policy: record May low deployed against US tariffs

INPE reported May 2026 Amazon deforestation 61.4% below May 2025 — the lowest May ever recorded — with the Aug–May cumulative down 37.5% at 2,189 km². Lula announced the figures June 11 at OTCA's Amazon Regional Observatory, with ministers explicitly rebutting Washington's environment-framed tariff measures.
Why it mattersBrasília is fusing enforcement results, Amazon-basin multilateralism (OTCA) and trade defence into one narrative. It strengthens Brazil's hand on TFFF delivery and post-Belém credibility — and tests whether environmental data can neutralize green-tinted protectionism.

DR CongoHIGH

Mining audit results due June 15 — the institutional test of cobalt power

President Tshisekedi's audit of copper-cobalt export revenues reports initial findings June 15, after analysis showed record export volumes producing disproportionately little state revenue; a prior audit found ~$16.8bn underreported (2018–23). New rules mandate 60–100% revenue repatriation, quotas run on a consolidated H1 deadline, and the FIS RDC strategic fund channels earnings to development.
Why it mattersThe DRC is building the institutional plumbing of resource sovereignty — quotas, repatriation, audit, sovereign fund — while the CMOC TFM strike shows workers contesting who the model serves. The outcome shapes the template for mineral-rich states across the Copperbelt and beyond.
