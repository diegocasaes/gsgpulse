Hormuz Still Closed: Four Months After the Ceasefire, the Energy Shock That Remade Global South Vulnerability Persists
In late February 2026, coordinated US–Israeli strikes on Iran triggered the closure of the Strait of Hormuz — the chokepoint through which roughly 20% of the world's seaborne oil trade and significant LNG volumes flow. A ceasefire was announced on April 8, but as of mid-August, ship traffic through the strait remains far below pre-war levels, and Iranian leadership has signaled continued commitment to its strategic position on the waterway. The Philippine government declared a national energy emergency and embarked on emergency LNG contracting. Indonesia's political crisis deepened in part due to fuel price shocks feeding protest waves. India, which sources a substantial share of its oil from the Gulf, absorbed visible economic scarring. China — the world's largest oil importer, with over 90% of its Iran purchases now disrupted — has been forced to accelerate supply-chain diversification at pace.
What makes this the signal story of the moment is not the crisis itself — it is the structural map it has drawn of Global South energy dependency. The closure has accelerated three deep trends simultaneously: the search for alternative supply chains (redirecting demand toward African and Latin American energy producers), the political weaponization of energy by non-state and state actors alike (Houthis, Iran), and the urgency of the just energy transition as a security argument rather than merely a climate one. South Africa's just transition agenda, Africa's minerals moment, and Southeast Asia's supply chain realignment are all downstream of this event. It has also functioned as a geopolitical stress test — exposing which Global South states have institutional resilience and which are structurally exposed to a world that can no longer take maritime security for granted.
Why this story won: 5/5 score on all criteria. Cross-source confirmation from Brookings, Atlas Institute, Wikipedia's 2026 crisis article, Philippine and Indonesian national-level policy responses, and CGTN/Al Jazeera regional analysis. It cascades into every other major story in this edition — from Philippine LNG contracting and Indonesia's protest wave to DRC minerals demand, BRICS de-dollarization, and South Africa's just transition. It is temporally live (strait still blocked), structurally irreversible in its demand-redirection effects, and affects Asia, MENA, Africa, and Latin America simultaneously.

DRC · Congo High
Twin Crisis: Cobalt Export Ban + Uranium Contamination Probe in 48 Hours
On August 6, DRC banned exports of copper and cobalt concentrates. On August 7, the government announced a probe into allegations that up to 5,000 tonnes of uranium may have been embedded in cobalt exports between 2000 and 2024. The DRC controls 71% of global cobalt reserves and ~73% of current production, with annual export quotas of 96,600 metric tonnes set for 2026–27.
Why It Matters
The export ban is the DRC's strongest resource sovereignty move in years — signaling that Kinshasa is willing to accept short-term revenue shocks to force renegotiation of value terms. The uranium probe, if confirmed, creates an IAEA-level governance crisis that could trigger sanctions or supply chain audits across global battery manufacturers. Both moves simultaneously.

Brazil High
Amazon Deforestation Falls 37% to 13-Year Low — With a Super El Niño on the Horizon
Brazil's INPE released data on August 7 showing Amazon deforestation alerts fell 36% year-on-year and are 55.6% below the preceding 10-year average. Lula used the occasion — INPE's 65th anniversary — to restate his 2030 zero illegal deforestation commitment. But forecasters are projecting a "super" El Niño through H2 2026, threatening drought and criminal fires across the basin.
Why It Matters
This is Lula's most measurable climate result heading into October elections — and it gives Brazil genuine diplomatic capital in COP31 negotiations. But the El Niño risk exposes how fragile forest governance remains when confronted with climate variability. The combination of a deforestation headline and a fire-season crisis in the same election cycle is politically volatile.

Philippines High
Energy Emergency Meets Geopolitical Obligation: Manila Caught Between Hormuz and the South China Sea
The Philippines declared a national energy emergency following the Hormuz closure in March 2026 and has been engaged in emergency LNG contracting ever since. Simultaneously, the country manages ongoing South China Sea tensions with China over Iroquois Reef and its treaty obligations to the US. Manila is the Southeast Asian state most exposed to both the energy shock and the US-China strategic competition.
Why It Matters
The Philippines is a stress test for what happens when a Global South state's geopolitical alignment creates energy vulnerability. US alliance commitments do not come with energy security guarantees. This is forcing Manila to diversify suppliers and accelerate renewables — but on an emergency timeline, with full costs externalized to Filipino households and industry.

Pakistan High
Pakistan's 79th Independence Day Under the Makkah Pact: New Regional Architecture or Overextension?
Pakistan signed the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement on August 7, 2026, alongside Saudi Arabia and Turkey. The treaty commits all three to treat an attack on one as an attack on all. Combined: 1.4M active personnel, 3,400 aircraft, 6,000 tanks, 340+ naval assets. Pakistan's Foreign Minister described the pact as "purely defensive" and open to other regional members.
Why It Matters
Pakistan is repositioning from a state struggling with economic crisis and Taliban overspill to a pivotal node in a new Muslim-majority security architecture. The pact sits at the junction of China's CPEC investment, Gulf petrodollar dependency, and NATO-adjacent Turkey. How India reads this — and whether New Delhi responds — will define South Asian security geometry through the next election cycle.

Indonesia High
Martial Law Enters Indonesian Public Discourse as Prabowo Faces Energy-Fueled Protest Wave
Sections of the Indonesian government have floated martial law in response to ongoing protest waves, partly fueled by fuel price shocks from the Hormuz closure. Parliament approved military deployment to civilian posts in March 2026. President Prabowo is consolidating power while facing an increasingly mobilized opposition. The 2025–26 Indonesian protests — which turned lethal after the death of protester Affan Kurniawan — remain an open wound.
Why It Matters
Indonesia is the world's largest Muslim-majority democracy and a middle power with significant weight in ASEAN and G20. Its democratic backsliding matters far beyond its borders. The energy shock is accelerating a political crisis that was already structural — and if martial law is imposed, it would remove Indonesia from any credible role as a regional democracy anchor ahead of COP31 in Australia.

India Med
India Holds BRICS Presidency, Proposes CBDC Interoperability, and Navigates Cautious De-Dollarization
With New Delhi's BRICS summit scheduled for September 12–13, India is proposing to link member-state central bank digital currencies (e-Rupee, China's digital yuan, Brazil's DREX, UAE dirham CBDC) as a core agenda item. Over 85% of BRICS+ mutual trade now settles in local currencies. India's External Affairs Minister Jaishankar simultaneously stated India has no policy to replace the dollar.
Why It Matters
India is threading a needle — advancing payment infrastructure that reduces dollar dependency while publicly maintaining credibility with Western financial institutions. This is sophisticated non-alignment in action. The CBDC interoperability framework, if it operationalizes at the New Delhi summit, would be the most concrete de-dollarization step BRICS has taken. Whether it survives US pressure is the open question.

South Africa Med
South Africa's Just Transition Under the Shadow of US Unilateralism and Hormuz Energy Shock
President Ramaphosa has reaffirmed the goal of over 40% clean energy by 2030, aligned with South Africa's 2025 Integrated Resource Plan. The JETP (Just Energy Transition Partnership) framework — anchored by G7 support — faces new uncertainty following US unilateralist moves. Meanwhile, the Hormuz closure has raised the strategic value of South Africa's own energy independence.
Why It Matters
South Africa is a JETP template for other African nations. If the G7 partnership frays — through US withdrawal or conditionality — the model breaks at precisely the moment when African nations most need it. South Africa's government is now framing the transition as an economic competitiveness strategy: a hedge against both fossil fuel volatility and G7 reliability.

Pacific Islands · Tuvalu / Palau / Fiji High
Pacific Islands Force the COP31 Pre-Agenda From the Pacific Itself — With NASA Science Confirming Irreversible Rise
Palau is hosting climate events at the Pacific Islands Forum in August 2026. World leaders have been invited to Fiji, Palau, and Tuvalu ahead of COP31 in Australia to witness climate destruction firsthand. NASA has confirmed that Pacific island nations will see at least 15cm of sea level rise in 30 years regardless of future emissions — meaning the question is no longer whether these islands flood, but whether the world will pay.

These nations are now building a legal, diplomatic, and moral case for loss-and-damage payments at scale — which will be the defining political fight of COP31.

Global · Indigenous Peoples Med
Indigenous Leaders Give UN a 12-Point Plan; CBD Nairobi Meeting Addresses Mineral Extraction on Territories
Indigenous leaders from around the world presented a 12-point plan to the UN in early August 2026 calling for sweeping action on land, rights, and lives. The CBD meeting in Nairobi (July 27–August 1) addressed grievance mechanisms for Indigenous peoples in protected areas. The 12-point UN plan and the CBD Nairobi engagement represent Indigenous movements building institutional leverage, not just advocacy. The FPIC obligation is the pressure point: if it's enforced, it reshapes where and how critical minerals can be extracted.

Global · AI Governance High
WAICO: 29 Nations Build AI Governance Body Outside G7 Frameworks — China Headquarters, No Western Members
The World Artificial Intelligence Cooperation Organization (WAICO) was formally established in Shanghai in July 2026, with 29 nations — including Pakistan, Indonesia, Kazakhstan, Laos, Russia — signing the founding agreement. No G7 members are represented. The Geneva-based Global South AI Summit (Aug 12–14) hosted 500+ delegates from 150+ nations. Open-source models like DeepSeek and Qwen are positioned as sovereignty tools.
Why It Matters
WAICO is the sharpest institutional expression yet of Global South digital sovereignty as a geopolitical project rather than a development goal. A China-headquartered intergovernmental AI body, with a mandate to reduce global AI capability gaps and no G7 participation, creates a parallel governance lane that will complicate UN AI governance processes. The open-source angle is key — it gives lower-income states meaningful capability without platform dependency.
